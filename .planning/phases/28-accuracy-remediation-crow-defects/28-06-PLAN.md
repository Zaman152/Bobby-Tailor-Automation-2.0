<objective>
Make the residual run-to-run variance impossible to mistake for a settled number.

Purpose: two identical invocations of the same pipeline over the same PDF produced **113 and 124 raw
items**, and three of fifty-one published quantities moved — `Door Frames-HM` 4.0 to blank, `Lift`
blank to 1.0, `Stairs` 1.0 to 2.0. Phase 24 stabilised the naming layer and proved it: the published
`(item, unit)` key sets are identical and 34 unnamed-geometry candidates are bit-identical. This
variance is one layer upstream, in the per-sheet vision extraction — the model reading different
descriptions off the same pixels.

There is a clean discriminator already in the artifact, and it predicts the variance exactly: every
quantity that moved traces to a calculated row carrying **no** `provenance`, while the `geometry` and
`text_layer` rows hold their numbers. The classification is therefore not a guess about which rows
might be shaky — it is a testable claim over two runs of real evidence, and this plan tests it.

The ROADMAP offers two ways to satisfy criterion 5: stabilise it, or make its instability visible as
a flag. This plan does the second, and says why the first is out of scope here — stabilising the
extraction pass means sampling it repeatedly, which is a paid pass, and this phase may not spend API.
The disclosure is what makes "a silently different number" impossible while that stays true.

Output: a route on every row, a section in `summary.txt` a human will actually read, and a test that
holds the classification to the evidence.
</objective>

Measured FREE from both saved runs before this plan was written:

| fact | run A | run B |
|---|---|---|
| raw line items | 113 | 124 |
| calculated rows, `provenance: geometry` | 13 | 12 |
| calculated rows, `provenance: text_layer` | 1 | 1 |
| calculated rows, no `provenance` | 12 | 23 |
| published `(item, unit)` key sets | **identical**, 51 rows, 0 only-in-A, 0 only-in-B |
| published quantities that DIFFER | 3: `Door Frames-HM` 4.0→None · `Lift` None→1.0 · `Stairs` 1.0→2.0 |
| provenance of every differing row's backing calculated rows | **absent in both runs** |
| unnamed-geometry candidates | 34 shared, bit-identical |

The `geometry` partition is not bit-identical between the runs, and the difference is instructive:
the SAME cluster is described as `personnel door` in A and `DOOR` in B, at the same count of 136.
The QUANTITY held; the DESCRIPTION moved. That is why the route classification keys on the recorded
provenance and never on the description text.

The limit that must be asserted rather than glossed: several no-provenance rows DID agree across the
two runs. So "unstable route" predicts "may move", not "did move", and the converse implication —
that agreement proves stability — is not available from two samples and must not be claimed.
</measured_facts>
