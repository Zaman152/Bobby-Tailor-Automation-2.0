<objective>
Stop one measured footprint from backing three published rows.

Purpose: `397,600 SF` publishes three times — `Exposed Structure` (MEASURED), `Sealed Concrete`
(MEASURED) and `Concrete Pad` (NEEDS REVIEW). An estimator who sums the SF column prices the
building three times. Two of the three are golden-correct; the third is a hypothesis.

The distinction that resolves it is already computed and already printed in the report. `Exposed
Structure` and `Sealed Concrete` publish with `source: plan_schedule_legend` and the formula
`authoritative plan schedule (397600.0 SF)` — the plan's own legend names them and states the area.
`Concrete Pad` publishes with `source: library` and its own review reason opens *"this row's NAME is
a hypothesis"*. It never measured a slab; it inherited the footprint because its measure basis is
area-shaped. That is precisely the sentence `_arbitrate_one_area_one_item` already writes when it
suppresses a layerless row — it just stops short of applying it to a row that names a layer.

Phase 23's D1 exception is not being taken back. Slab, floor finish and roof structure genuinely
share a plan area. What changes is that a row must have printed evidence for its NAME before it may
claim one of those layers.

The reason this takes three files is that the region identity is dropped between fusion and the
report, so today the aggregator could only tell the three rows apart by noticing their numbers are
equal. Arbitrating on numeric equality would be a coincidence rule, and this phase rejects those.

Output: `region_ref` threaded end to end, and an arbitration that keeps declared layer sharing while
refusing a hypothesis a layer it did not measure.
</objective>
